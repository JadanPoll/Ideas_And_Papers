# Recursive Determinant Matrices (RDM)

### A working notation for fine-grained matrix structure, and the real mathematics it turns out to touch

*Working notes, not a finished theory. The notation was intuited independently, then traced back to where it already exists — in whole or in fragments — across group theory, gauge theory, dynamical systems, and a handful of other fields. The goal of this document is to preserve every real connection found so far in one place, cleanly enough that it can be picked back up and pushed further at any point in the future.*

---

## 1. Motivation and core notation

Standard matrix notation treats an entry `a_ij` as an irreducible scalar. RDM asks: what if some entries are allowed to be more than that — a scalar that is secretly the determinant of a hidden `k×k` sub-matrix, expandable on demand for finer-grained manipulation, while still behaving like an ordinary number whenever you don't need the extra detail?

**Core definitions**

- **Expansion operator**: `E_ij(A) = M_ij`, a `k×k` matrix living "inside" position `(i,j)` of `A`.
- **Determinant-preserving constraint**: `a_ij = det(M_ij)`. The coarse view is always recoverable from the fine view.
- **Ω tag**: originally proposed as a naive recursion-depth counter. Redefined over the course of this exploration into something much more useful — see §9.

The rest of this document is the record of what happens when you actually push on this structure.

---

## 2. Where this already exists, in fragments, in known mathematics

Before building anything new, it's worth being honest about the precedents, because several of them supply exactly the machinery RDM needs:

- **Quasideterminants** (Gelfand & Retakh) — recursive, submatrix-and-inverse-based determinant analogues for noncommutative rings. The closest existing formal cousin of RDM's core idea.
- **Plücker coordinates / the Grassmannian** — a family of determinants (maximal minors) that aren't independent, but satisfy consistency relations so the whole family describes one coherent object. The real precedent for needing *some* global consistency constraint (what Ω was originally reaching for).
- **Block matrices and the Schur complement** — ordinary linear algebra already lets entries be matrices, with exact formulas relating the whole determinant to blocks and their inverses.
- **Hierarchical matrices (H-matrices)** — numerical-analysis structures that recursively partition a matrix and represent different blocks at different resolutions, exactly the coarse/fine duality RDM wants, motivated by computational speed rather than determinant bookkeeping.
- **Dodgson condensation / the Desnanot–Jacobi identity** — a genuine identity nesting determinants of overlapping minors inside a larger determinant.
- **Tensor networks (MPS/MPO, MERA)** — physics constructions where a single "effective" tensor at a coarse scale stands in for a whole contracted sub-network at a finer scale.

None of these is RDM outright. All of them hand it working parts.

## 3. Choosing the "shape" of a perturbation via Lie groups

**The central mechanism**: `det: G → ℂ*` is a group homomorphism for any matrix Lie group `G ⊆ GL(k,ℂ)`. Restricting `M_ij` to live inside a specific `G` automatically restricts `a_ij = det(M_ij)` to live inside `det(G)` — no extra bookkeeping required.

| `M_ij` lives in | forces `a_ij` to be |
|---|---|
| `U(k)` (unitary) | on the unit circle, `U(1)` |
| `O(k)` (orthogonal) | `{+1, −1}` — a literal two-valued oscillation |
| `SU(k)` / `Sp(2k)` | fixed at exactly `1` — internal motion, invisible externally |
| `GL⁺(k,ℝ)` | any positive real — pure scaling |

**Infinitesimal version — Jacobi's formula**: `d(det M) = det(M)·tr(M⁻¹ dM)`. If the perturbation is generated by a Hermitian `H` (`dM = iH·M dt`, the standard way to move inside `U(k)`), this reduces to `d(det M)/det(M) = i·tr(H)·dt` — purely imaginary, which is exactly why a unitary perturbation's determinant is confined to the circle rather than drifting off it.

**The `SU(k)` case is an isospectral flow** — the same family of structures as the Toda lattice: real internal motion, zero macro-visible effect. This becomes the "geometric / flat" entry in the final five-regime taxonomy (§14).

**Named real objects for the "consistency tracking" job Ω was reaching for**: determinant line bundles (used in the Quillen metric, index theory, anomaly cancellation) are the formal geometric machinery for tracking a varying determinant continuously, with its own connection and curvature.

## 4. The multiplication / fusion rule

For `A` (m×n) and `B` (n×p) with expanded entries, `(AB)_il = Σ_j a_ij·b_jl = Σ_j det(M_ij)·det(N_jl)`. Because determinant is multiplicative for same-size square matrices:

**`(AB)_il = Σ_j det(M_ij · N_jl)`**

The "interaction between perturbations" isn't a new axiom — it's ordinary matrix multiplication of the internal blocks, summed over the shared index. Call this the **fusion rule**.

**Group closure theorem**: if `M_ij, N_jl ∈ G` (a Lie group), then `M_ij·N_jl ∈ G` too, for free, by group closure. Whatever shape constraint you chose in §3 survives multiplication automatically.

**The non-commutativity observation (the strongest single result in this exploration)**: at the coarse level, `a_ij·b_jl = b_jl·a_ij` — scalars always commute. Internally, `M_ij·N_jl ≠ N_jl·M_ij` in general. RDM structurally produces a non-commuting hidden layer underneath a commuting visible layer — the same relationship quantum mechanics has to its classical limit. This has an exact theorem underneath it:

> For `n ≥ 2` over essentially any field of interest, `SL(n)` is the commutator subgroup of `GL(n)`, and `GL(n)/SL(n) ≅ F*` via `det` is the abelianization map.

So the internal fluctuations invisible to the macro determinant (the `SU(k)`/`SL(k)` case above) are *exactly* the non-abelian part of the structure group. This single fact unifies §3's isospectral case with the non-commutativity intuition.

**Dimension mismatch**: if `M_ij` and `N_jl` have different sizes, you don't get genuine fusion for free — only the trivial non-interacting case `det(M)det(N) = det(M⊕N)` (block-diagonal, any dimensions). Real interaction across mismatched dimensions requires an extra coupling datum: an **intertwiner**, exactly as in representation theory (Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for combining representations of different dimension). The special/interesting case is when the mismatch *resolves* into equal dimensions: this is precisely a **degeneracy locus** in algebraic geometry (governed by the **Thom–Porteous formula**), and when the two dimensions match exactly, the map becomes an honest section of a line bundle whose zero locus is a plain divisor with a one-line Chern-class formula. "Square is the interesting case" is not a coincidence — it's the case where a generalized determinant becomes well-defined again.

**Correction, since we're standardizing**: rectangular composition does *not* break associativity — `(MN)P = M(NP)` whenever dimensions line up, always. What actually changes is *typing*: composition is only defined when source/target dimensions match. That's the category **Mat** (objects = dimensions, morphisms = matrices) — "direction" in RDM is a morphism's source→target arrow, not a lost algebra axiom.

**Where real non-associativity does live**: the Cayley–Dickson tower `ℝ → ℂ → ℍ → 𝕆`, each doubling costing one property (order, commutativity, associativity). The exact matrix-with-determinant analogue at the end of that tower is the **exceptional Jordan algebra** — `3×3` Hermitian matrices with octonionic entries — which carries its own cubic "determinant" (Freudenthal's generic norm), symmetry group `F₄`, and sits at the base of Freudenthal's magic square connecting to `E₆, E₇, E₈`. This is the genuine home for a fully non-associative RDM extension, if one is ever wanted.

**Addition, honestly flagged**: the original proposal's `E_ij(A+B) = E_ij(A) ⊕ E_ij(B)` doesn't work as written — `det(M⊕N) = det(M)·det(N)`, a product, not a sum. Determinant is a multiplicative homomorphism, not an additive one; this is a real obstruction, not an oversight to patch trivially. The closest honest tool for the additive direction is the **matrix determinant lemma**: `det(M + uvᵀ) = det(M)·(1 + vᵀM⁻¹u)` — the correct starting point for a future additive-fusion rule, not direct sum.

## 5. Holonomy as the organizing "force" concept

**The reinterpretation that ties the whole notation to real physics**: `a_ij` alone is not gauge-invariant — it depends on an arbitrary local basis choice at `i` and `j`. What *is* invariant is the loop product `M_ij · N_jl · P_li` around a closed cycle. This is the **holonomy**, and its infinitesimal version is **curvature** — which is, literally and not metaphorically, the force in:

- **Yang–Mills gauge theory**: `F_μν = ∂A_ν − ∂A_μ + [A_μ,A_ν]`, the field strength, is the infinitesimal holonomy of the gauge connection. The commutator term is exactly why non-abelian forces (weak, strong) are richer than electromagnetism.
- **General relativity**: the Riemann curvature tensor is the holonomy of the Levi-Civita connection around infinitesimal loops; tidal forces and geodesic deviation are its direct manifestation.
- **Lattice gauge theory (Wilson, 1974)**: put a group-valued matrix on each edge of a discretized spacetime; the *only* physical content is loop products (plaquettes). This is, essentially, RDM's mathematical skeleton, restricted to one Lie group per edge.

**Governing theorem**: the **Ambrose–Singer theorem** — the full holonomy algebra at a point is generated by the curvature transported in from every loop based there.

**The minimal problem that forces the notation into existence**: 2D holonomy (Gauss's surveying problem — triangle angle deficit = enclosed curvature, Gauss–Bonnet) only ever needs a *scalar*, because 2D rotational holonomy lives in the abelian group `U(1)`. **Matrices become mandatory exactly at 3D**, because transporting a full frame around a loop on a curved 3-manifold gives a rotation in `SO(3)` — which doesn't commute. This is the same non-commuting group whose composition-of-rotations problem drove Hamilton to invent quaternions. Minimal statement: *carry a rotating reference frame around a closed path in a curved 3-space, and ask whether it returns to where it started.*

**A live, hard, real problem in this exact neighborhood** (offered as inspiration, not a claim of tractability): the Yang–Mills existence and mass gap problem, a Clay Millennium Prize problem, attacked using precisely this lattice/Wilson-loop formalism.

## 6. Continuous form — the Lie-algebra / generator picture

Let `M_ij` flow: `dM_ij/dt = A_ij(t)·M_ij(t)`, `A_ij(t)` an element of the Lie algebra (Lie's infinitesimal generator; the gauge connection restricted to the edge). The solution is the path-ordered exponential `M_ij(t) = 𝒫exp(∫₀ᵗ A_ij(s)ds)` — the continuous version of the discrete loop product.

Applying Jacobi's formula to this flow gives a single identity that unifies nearly everything above:

**Liouville's formula: `d(ln det M_ij)/dt = tr(A_ij(t))`**

- `A_ij` traceless (`su(k)`/`sl(k)`) → `det` exactly constant → the isospectral/"invisible motion" case, now *derived* rather than asserted.
- `A_ij` anti-Hermitian (`u(k)`) → `tr(A_ij)` purely imaginary → the phase of `a_ij` advances at that rate; integrated around a closed loop, this gives the winding number / Berry phase (§8), with an explicit formula `w = (1/2π)∮ Im(tr A) dt`.

## 7. The degenerate case: det = 0

At `det(M_ij) = 0`, the polar decomposition `M = UP` stops being unique — `U` is no longer pinned down on the kernel direction. This ambiguity is the mathematical signature of:

- A **diabolical point** (von Neumann & Wigner) — a genuine parameter-space degeneracy — or, non-Hermitian setting, an **exceptional point**.
- A **monopole source of curvature** (Berry): loops encircling the degeneracy pick up a phase proportional to the solid angle subtended, geometrically identical to a Dirac monopole. Liouville's formula explains why: near `det = 0`, `ln det → −∞`, forcing `tr(A)` to develop a pole exactly there — the generator's own Dirac string.
- The curved-spacetime analogue: the **Raychaudhuri equation** is the exact curved-spacetime version of Liouville's formula (expansion `θ = tr` of the generator); `θ → −∞` is a **caustic** — the physical incarnation of `det → 0` in gravitational lensing.

`det = 0` loci are where curvature has genuine sources, not where the notation breaks.

## 8. Two distinct reset mechanisms — do not conflate

### 8a. Temporal reset — Benettin's algorithm (Lyapunov exponents)

A continuous flow `M(t)` numerically overflows or collapses in finite time. Fix: periodically factor `M(t_n) = Q_n R_n` (`Q` unitary, `R` upper-triangular, positive diagonal), **reset the working matrix to `Q_n`**, and separately accumulate `ln(diag R_n)`. The running total, divided by elapsed time, gives the Lyapunov exponents. This is a real, named, standard technique — used precisely because raw tangent flows are numerically unusable without it.

### 8b. Spatial reset — maximal tree gauge fixing (lattice gauge theory)

This is the one actually being asked for: **choose the resetting term to be exactly the identity, not merely unitary, and keep stacking.**

**Governing fact**: any single non-looping chain of composed terms can always be gauge-fixed to `I` — a path with no closed loop carries zero intrinsic (gauge-invariant) content, so the local basis can always be chosen to absorb the transport entirely. This stops working *only* the instant a new term reconnects to a previously visited index, closing a cycle — that closing edge cannot generally be forced to `I`, and its deviation from `I` is exactly the holonomy of §5.

**Named real technique**: **maximal tree gauge fixing** (axial/temporal gauge in lattice gauge theory). Given a network of indices, pick a spanning tree; there is always exactly enough local gauge freedom (one choice per node) to force every tree edge to `I`, for free. The only physically meaningful data left is the small number of leftover **chord** edges that close cycles — each one, combined with the (now-trivial) tree path connecting its endpoints, reproduces exactly a loop holonomy.

**Degrees-of-freedom theorem**: the true count of independent physical content in an RDM structure is not the number of expanded elements — it is the number of independent cycles in the underlying graph, `E − (V − 1)` for a connected graph on `V` vertices and `E` edges (the graph's cycle rank / first Betti number). Everything else is bookkeeping you're free to discard.

## 9. The redefined Ω tag

Given §6–8, Ω is no longer a naive recursion-depth counter. At each edge:

- A generator field `A_ij(t)` in a chosen Lie algebra `𝔤` (the shape law of §3, now infinitesimal)
- The flow `M_ij(t)`, giving `a_ij(t) = det(M_ij(t))` as the instantaneous coarse view
- A reset schedule distinguishing **temporal resets** (§8a, for numerical stability of a flow) from **spatial/gauge resets** (§8b, for removing redundant non-physical freedom across a network)
- An accumulator recording, per edge or per independent cycle: total log-magnitude drift and total phase winding, surviving resets even as the working matrix is renormalized

Ω is this accumulator — a pair (magnitude memory, phase memory) — not a depth counter.

## 10. Six-field reinterpretation — bookkeeping tensions this notation already speaks to

| Field | Coarse scalar (`a_ij`) | Hidden fine structure (`M_ij`) | What "loop closure" means | Existing real fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantum chemistry | Entry of a reduced density matrix | Full N-electron wavefunction it was traced from | Whether a proposed reduced matrix could have come from *any* valid wavefunction | **N-representability problem** (the field's actual name for RDM, coincidentally) |
| Robotics (kinematic chains) | One joint's transform | Full 6-DOF rigid transform | Whether a closed mechanism's chained transforms return to identity | Loop-closure constraints (parallel robots, four-bar linkages) |
| SLAM / mapping | Estimated relative pose | The full noisy driven trajectory | Whether revisiting a place closes back to identity | Loop-closure detection + pose-graph optimization |
| Deep learning | Jacobian determinant per layer | Full weight matrix at that layer | Whether repeated composition over depth stays bounded | Vanishing/exploding gradients; orthogonal weights, layer/batch norm (= reset) |
| Structural engineering (FEA) | Effective condensed stiffness | Full fine-mesh stiffness matrix | Whether the condensed model reproduces full-mesh boundary behavior | Static condensation / Guyan reduction (a Schur complement) |
| General relativity | det of geodesic deviation tensor | Full curvature tensor along the congruence | Whether transport around a small loop returns a frame unchanged | Holonomy; Raychaudhuri focusing, caustics |

**The unified template** these all share: (1) name the coarse scalar you actually want to reason about, (2) name the fine structure it was built from, (3) determine whether the relationships form a network or a simple chain, (4) pick a spanning tree of that network and gauge-fix it to identity for free, (5) the leftover chords are the only true, irreducible degrees of freedom.

## 11. A third scaling regime: marginal / fractional

Liouville's formula (§6) gives three regimes depending on `tr(A)`:

- **Pole**: `tr(A)` fixed, sign-definite → exponential blowup/collapse
- **Geometric (isospectral)**: `tr(A) = 0` exactly → flat, invisible motion
- **Marginal**: `tr(A) → 0` asymptotically, not identically → fractional / anomalous scaling

**Direct link to §8a's own machinery**: order the Lyapunov exponents `λ₁ ≥ ... ≥ λₙ`; find the largest `j` with `λ₁+...+λⱼ ≥ 0`; then

`D = j + (λ₁+...+λⱼ)/|λⱼ₊₁|`

is the **Kaplan–Yorke dimension** — generically non-integer, and (Kaplan–Yorke conjecture, proven in many cases) equal to the actual fractal dimension of a strange attractor. A genuinely fractional-dimensional scalar, produced by the exact same accumulator already built for §8a.

**General mechanism**: renormalization-group **marginal operators** — the borderline where naive scaling gives exactly zero, and next-order corrections resum (via the Callan–Symanzik equation) into an anomalous, fractional exponent. Canonical worked example: the **Kosterlitz–Thouless transition**.

**Cross-eigenbasis "pressure" — two distinct real mechanisms**:
1. **Center manifold slaving**: a marginal eigendirection forces every other direction's long-term dynamics to become a function of it (center manifold theorem) — a precise, provable form of one dimension pressuring the rest.
2. **Non-normal / pseudospectral coupling**: even without resonance, non-orthogonal eigenvectors let one "safely decaying" direction transiently amplify another before eventual decay (the real mechanism behind unexpected turbulence onset in flows whose eigenvalues individually look stable).

## 12. A fourth regime: oscillatory / imaginary bookkeeping

**A latent example already in the framework**: `M_ij ∈ O(k)` gives `det ∈ {+1,−1}` — a period-2 sign oscillation, built in since §3.

**A concrete buildable definition for a period-4 generalization**: seed a twist at recursion depth `n`: `a_ij⁽ⁿ⁾ = iⁿ · det(M_ij⁽ⁿ⁾)`. Depth 0 real, depth 1 imaginary, depth 2 negative real, depth 3 negative imaginary, depth 4 back to real — the cyclic group `{1,i,−1,−i} ≅ ℤ/4ℤ`.

**Three real precedents for exactly this**:
- **The Maslov index**: at every classical turning point (a `det = 0` crossing in the WKB sense), the connection formula picks up an explicit factor of `i` (a quarter-turn phase); the accumulated integer count mod 4 is a required correction term in real semiclassical quantization (Bohr–Sommerfeld).
- **The Pfaffian**: for antisymmetric `M`, `det(M) = Pf(M)²` — a literal "square root of the determinant" that flips sign under basis permutation, exactly one recursive layer beneath the scalar view.
- **Clifford algebras / Bott periodicity**: generators built from `eᵢ² = −1` (a sign flip baked in by construction); adding generators cycles the algebra's real/complex/quaternionic character with period 8 — the actual machinery underneath `ℝ → ℂ → ℍ → 𝕆`.

## 13. A fifth regime: damped oscillatory (the Galois connection)

**Discrete recursion `aₙ = λ·aₙ₋₁` sign behavior by `λ`**:

| `λ` | behavior |
|---|---|
| `λ > 1` | pole |
| `0 < λ < 1` | geometric decay, no flip |
| `λ = −1` | pure undamped oscillation (§12's `O(k)` case) |
| `−1 < λ < 0` | **decays and flips sign at every level** |

A key structural fact: a *continuous* flow `M(t) = e^{At}` can never produce sign-flipping decay from a real eigenvalue alone — that requires a genuinely complex eigenvalue (a damped spiral). The *discrete*, level-by-level nature of RDM's recursion gets sign-flipping decay for free, from an ordinary negative number, because "one level deeper" is a jump, not a flow.

**A real historical precedent**: continued fraction convergents are built from continuant matrices `[[aₙ,1],[1,0]]`, each with `det = −1` exactly. The product of `n` of them has determinant exactly `(−1)ⁿ` — undamped sign-flipping, forever, because each factor is unimodular; this is the `GL(2,ℤ)` structure underneath the Euclidean algorithm. Évariste Galois, at seventeen, proved the exact periodicity condition for continued fraction expansions of reduced quadratic irrationals — genuinely working inside this structure. Notably, the classical continuant *never collapses*; what decays to zero is a separate quantity (the remainder sequence).

**The genuinely new construction found here**: fuse the two ingredients that the classical case keeps separate. Let each step contribute `−rᵢ` for `0 < rᵢ < 1` — simultaneously orientation-reversing and strictly contracting:

`aₙ = ∏ᵢ₌₁ⁿ (−rᵢ)`

A **damped continuant** — genuinely converges to 0 while flipping sign at every level. No standard name is known for this object; it is presented here as an open, well-posed construction.

## 15. Quasiparticles, entanglement, and decoherence as tree-gauge collapse

**The proposal**: build a quasiparticle from Pauli-matrix-like unitary sections `M_1, M_2` that are "allowed to evolve," entangled with each other, where `det(M_1 · M_2)` is only computed — and the state only collapses — at the moment of decoherence/measurement.

**A necessary correction first.** If `M_1, M_2 ∈ SU(2)` (Pauli-generated rotations in the usual sense), `det` is identically `1` forever — this is exactly the isospectral case of §3, already frozen from the start. For `det` to carry any information at all, the internal structure must live in `U(2)`, not `SU(2)`, so an overall phase is tracked.

**But an isolated overall phase is exactly the wrong place to look for collapse.** A single subsystem's global phase never affects Born-rule outcomes on its own — it is invisible by construction. The physics is not "det computed in isolation," but:

- **Entanglement turns global phase into relative phase.** Once `M_1` and `M_2`'s evolution is correlated with another part of a joint system, an otherwise-invisible determinant/phase becomes a genuine relative phase between branches of the joint state — exactly **phase kickback**: a controlled-`U` leaves an ancilla's own value looking untouched while kicking the phase of `U` into an observable relative phase across its superposition.
- **Decoherence is what freezes that relative phase into a classical read-out**, not the computation of a determinant per se. Tracing out an environment that has become entangled with the system (Zurek's **einselection**) is the real mechanism converting live relative-phase information into definite, classical statistics.

**In the document's own vocabulary**: `M_ij` should be `U(2)`-valued; while coherent, its evolving determinant is an inert phase; entangling two such sections creates a genuine **loop holonomy** (§5) across the network; and decoherence is the act of collapsing to a **tree gauge** (§8b) — fixing every non-measured edge to `I` and freezing the one remaining chord's holonomy into a single classical number. Measurement, in this language, is nothing but a forced choice of gauge.

**Where this already exists as real physics, closing a loop back to §12**:

- **Topological quantum computation with non-abelian anyons.** Quasiparticles are manipulated by **braiding** worldlines — a sequence of unitary operations, topologically protected from local noise, matching "allowed to evolve" — and read out only by **fusion**, the single point where anything collapses. Between braids there is no well-defined local state; the information lives entirely in the entangled, global structure.
- **Majorana zero modes.** Two Majoranas together form one ordinary fermion; individually, neither carries a local quantum number, only a shared, topologically protected parity — a physical quasiparticle with no well-defined local state on its own, exactly the proposal's structure.
- **The Pfaffian, again.** The invariant determining whether a physical system supports Majorana modes at all is the **sign of a Pfaffian** — the Read–Green / Kitaev ℤ₂ invariant. This is the same object introduced in §12 as "one recursive layer beneath the determinant, with its own sign flip" — here it turns out to be the load-bearing quantity behind a real, physically realized quasiparticle of exactly this collapse-only-at-fusion kind.

This section is presented as a genuinely promising extension, not yet a worked theorem — see the added item in §16.

## 17. Closing note

This document is a snapshot of an intuition, followed rigorously enough to discover that it independently reconstructs recognizable fragments of quasideterminant theory, Lie group representation theory, gauge-theoretic holonomy, dynamical systems' Lyapunov machinery, semiclassical quantum mechanics, continued fraction theory, and — most recently — the structure of topological quantum computation, without having set out to do any of that. Nothing here constitutes a new theorem; every named result cited is a real, existing piece of mathematics or physics. What is original is the specific act of noticing that all of it fits under one small notation, and the resulting taxonomy and problem set. Whether the notation "earns its keep" — produces a genuinely new result, rather than just a genuinely good index into old ones — is exactly §16, problem 4, and is left open.
