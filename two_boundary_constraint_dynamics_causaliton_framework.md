# Two-Boundary Constraint Dynamics

## Causalitons, Causal Closure, and the Layered Realization of Quantum Information

### A speculative framework for intuition compression, conceptual separation, and experimental question generation

**Status:** Preformal research-program essay  
**Primary purpose:** To crystallize an intuition-generating framework, not to claim an established mechanism  
**Working name:** Two-Boundary Constraint Dynamics (TBCD)  
**Provisional intuitive object:** the causaliton

---

## Abstract

This essay develops a speculative framework called **Two-Boundary Constraint Dynamics**. Its central proposal is not that a newly discovered particle literally travels backward through time. It is that several concepts routinely bundled together in quantum language should be separated:

\[
\text{phase-supported existence}
\neq
\text{causal realizability}
\neq
\text{record realization}
\neq
\text{classical objectivity}.
\]

The term **causaliton** is introduced as an intuition-bearing pseudoparticle or quasi-structure representing a phase-bearing contribution from possible later records toward their causal ancestry. The causaliton may ultimately correspond to a propagator contribution, a constraint kernel, a feature of an all-at-once boundary-value solution, an emergent relational variable, or something that cannot be faithfully represented through particle language at all. Its current value is heuristic: it makes relational distinctions manipulable before the correct mathematics is known.

The framework distinguishes three provisional causal statuses. A **classical causaliton** participates in a directly record-closing cause-effect history. A **virtual causaliton** reaches closure only after finite backdating through a causal hierarchy to an earlier common causal region. An **imaginary causaliton** has phase-supported or information-preserving status but no finite independently record-closing ancestry relative to the detector under consideration. The boundary between virtual and imaginary causation has a halting-like logical asymmetry: causal closure has a finite witness when found, while any finite failure to find closure does not prove permanent nonclosure.

The framework further proposes that macroscopic environments are not merely sources of noise or decoherence. They form nested layers of partial observation: atoms, molecules, fields, apparatus components, and environmental fragments acquire relational information and constrain the space of future records. Stable classical facts emerge when one source-environment-detector closure becomes redundantly encoded. Quantum probability, viewed only in the forward direction, is then interpreted as the structured appearance of phase information before complete causal and record closure.

The most testable conjecture is that future-conditioned constraint may possess a finite invariant resolution scale. If a source opens effective relational degrees of freedom faster than the backward-conditioned hierarchy can differentiate, settle, and incorporate them, the inherited causaliton group becomes temporarily less concentrated. Two protocols that end in the same complete quantum state and operational channel might then differ because one temporarily opened its unresolved branch structure more rapidly or through a deeper hierarchy. Reversible scrambling and exact uncomputation provide a clean family of tests.

No claim is made that this is the correct ontology of quantum mechanics. The framework is valuable only insofar as it compresses intuition, forces hidden assumptions into view, generates invariant definitions, and produces experiments or mathematical questions capable of proving it wrong.

## 1. Why Construct This Framework?

Quantum mechanics is extraordinarily successful as a predictive formalism. Its most familiar explanatory phrase, **wave-particle duality**, is far less successful as a conceptual decomposition. It tells us that quantum systems display wave-like and particle-like behavior, but it often leaves several distinct questions entangled:

1. What does the quantum state say exists?
2. What phase structure can contribute to interference?
3. What can causally influence a particular detector?
4. What can be attributed to a particular source?
5. What becomes a localized transfer of energy, momentum, charge, or spin?
6. What becomes a stable record?
7. What becomes redundantly accessible to many observers?
8. Why does the forward-time description require probabilities?
9. Why does a completed macroscopic history appear definite?

The framework began from a loose picture of “retrocausal refraction”: later settled structures were imagined as exerting a backward phase constraint, while rapid local expansion of degrees of freedom scattered or flattened the collective constraint. The original intuition was deliberately visual and underdefined. It used terms such as “radiation,” “group wave,” “refraction,” and “gray-area window.” The important progress was not preserving every literal image. It was discovering which distinctions repeatedly survived correction.

One early durable insight was that a static number of degrees of freedom was not enough. What mattered was an active race between opening and settlement: a system could temporarily expand unresolved possibilities faster than those possibilities became fixed records, but once the new state settled it would itself become part of the constraint structure. The original notes explicitly shifted attention from static complexity to the rate of increasing degrees of freedom and to the later formation of a new retrocausal boundary. The notes also emphasized a cascade of nested temporal levels rather than a direct jump from a distant future to an unconstrained present.

The mature framework therefore does not begin with consciousness, free will, or a special biological exception. Its mechanism, if any, must be universal. Conscious systems may eventually be studied as unusually recursive local architectures, but atoms, molecules, cavities, detectors, stars, and black holes must obey the same basic relational rules.

The project has two independent standards of success:

- **Interpretive success:** Does the framework separate concepts and compress intuition more effectively than “wave-particle duality” alone?
- **Physical success:** Does it define invariant quantities and predict something not already contained in standard quantum theory, quantum field theory, open-system dynamics, or general relativity?

The first standard has already produced useful questions. The second remains entirely open.

## 2. Epistemic Status and Methodological Commitments

This document is not a proclamation of a discovered theory. It is a **preformal research program**.

The literal causaliton mechanism may be wrong in any of several ways:

- there may be no fundamental causaliton field;
- backward propagation may be only an all-at-once boundary condition;
- “propagation time” may be an artifact of an iterative solver;
- the causaliton may reduce to a propagator, influence functional, adjoint state, or conditional amplitude;
- the three causaliton sectors may be useful classifications but not independent physical species;
- apparent flattening may reduce to ordinary decoherence, scrambling, detector bandwidth, or non-Markovian memory;
- the proposed relation to time dilation may be only an analogy;
- “imaginary causalitons” may be empirically empty;
- no deviation from standard quantum mechanics may exist.

The framework should therefore preserve a strict distinction between:

\[
\text{durable conceptual separation}
\]

and

\[
\text{provisional mechanism}.
\]

A useful heuristic object can disappear during formalization while the distinctions it revealed remain. The history of physics contains many such cases: rays, phonons, holes, quasiparticles, effective fields, and path-integral histories may be indispensable at one level without being fundamental microscopic objects.

### 2.1 The primary methodological rule

Every intuitive term should eventually answer one of four questions:

1. **What invariant quantity does it represent?**
2. **What existing formal object is its nearest analogue?**
3. **What observation would distinguish it from that analogue?**
4. **What result would falsify or eliminate it?**

### 2.2 What the framework does not assume

It does not assume:

- a preferred inertial frame;
- that spacelike intervals can become timelike under a Lorentz transformation;
- that observers must be conscious;
- that every nonzero wavefunction tail is a tiny teleportation probability;
- that normalization and renormalization are the same;
- that all quantum uncertainty is merely ignorance;
- that decoherence alone selects one ontic outcome;
- that backward causation permits backward signaling;
- that causal closure is literally Turing-undecidable;
- that the future can be bilked or contradicted;
- that local competition overrides global consistency.

## 3. The Core Separation

The central architecture is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_D
\subseteq
\mathcal C_D
\subseteq
\mathcal E
}
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal E\) is the total existential or phase-supported structure;
- \(\mathcal C_D\) is the structure causally realizable relative to detector or record system \(D\);
- \(\mathcal R_D\) is the structure actually realized as a stable record at \(D\).

A further subset describes stable public classical facts:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O
\subseteq
\mathcal R_D
\subseteq
\mathcal C_D
\subseteq
\mathcal E,
}
\]

where \(\mathcal O\) denotes redundantly recorded, effectively objective structure.

This single nesting separates several claims that ordinary language often treats as interchangeable.

### 3.1 Existential or phase-supported structure

A contribution has existential support if it is required by the complete invariant state, phase relation, conservation law, or global correlation structure.

Existential support does not automatically mean that the contribution can be isolated as an event. It may be required because removing it would change:

- interference;
- normalization;
- entanglement;
- a spectral sum rule;
- a conserved charge;
- a global gauge-invariant phase;
- or the consistency of the complete history.

A schematic measure is:

\[
E(A)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P_A),
\]

where \(P_A\) identifies a state-space sector. The framework does not assume that \(E(A)\) is itself the probability of an independently realizable detector event.

### 3.2 Causal realizability

Causal realizability is relational. It asks whether a source intervention can alter a detector’s statistics through an admissible physical history.

A response-based quantity is:

\[
C_{S\rightarrow D}
=
\frac{\delta \langle M_D\rangle}{\delta J_S},
\]

where \(J_S\) controls a source operation and \(M_D\) is a detector observable.

If \(C_{S\rightarrow D}=0\), changing the source cannot change that detector’s outcome in the arrangement under consideration, even when the global state contains nonzero phase or correlation structure associated with both regions.

### 3.3 Causal attributability

A detector can click without the click being reliably attributable to a particular source. Define:

\[
A_{S\rightarrow D}
=
\frac12
\left\|
\rho_D^{(S_1)}-\rho_D^{(S_0)}
\right\|_1.
\]

This compares the detector states under two controlled source preparations. A click probability may be nonzero because of detector noise, vacuum fluctuations, environmental sources, or unrelated particles, while the source-attributable difference remains zero.

### 3.4 Temporal localization

A relation may be causally attributable but distributed across a broad temporal history. Let \(r_{S\rightarrow D}(t)\) be the detector response attributable to source interventions around time \(t\). Define a width:

\[
L_{S\rightarrow D}^2
=
\frac{
\int (t-\bar t)^2 |r(t)|^2\,dt
}{
\int |r(t)|^2\,dt
}.
\]

A small \(L\) means that cause and effect can be localized in a narrow interval. A large \(L\) means the causal relationship is historically distributed or difficult to date from the observer’s forward description.

### 3.5 Record realization

A record is not merely a phase correlation. It is a physical encoding stable enough to affect later systems. A detector effect \(M_D\) gives:

\[
R_D=\operatorname{Tr}(M_D\rho_D).
\]

The record may still be private or fragile. Classical objectivity requires redundancy.

### 3.6 Classical objectivity

Let \(E_k\) denote environmental fragments. A redundancy count can be written schematically as:

\[
\Omega(X)
=
\sum_k
\mathbf 1
\left[
I(X:E_k)\ge (1-\epsilon)H(X)
\right].
\]

Large \(\Omega\) means many fragments independently carry nearly complete information about the same robust observable. This is the environment-as-witness and quantum-Darwinism picture: objective classical facts are those copied widely enough that many observers can discover them without directly perturbing the original system [6].

## 4. What Is a Causaliton?

A **causaliton** is a provisional representation of a phase-bearing contribution from possible record closure toward the causal ancestry compatible with that record.

It should not be pictured too literally. It may correspond to:

- an advanced or adjoint propagator contribution;
- a backward message in a forward-backward inference algorithm;
- a constraint multiplier in a boundary-value problem;
- a component of a two-state-vector description;
- a quasiparticle-like excitation of an emergent relational field;
- an influence-functional contribution;
- a term in a globally constrained classical field;
- or an object that exists only in configuration or history space.

The causaliton is therefore **load-bearing intuition**, not established ontology.

### 4.1 Forward and backward amplitudes

A minimal toy structure is:

\[
F_{k+1}(x')
=
\int K(x'|x)F_k(x)\,dx,
\]

\[
R_k(x)
=
\int K(x'|x)
S_{k+1}(x')
R_{k+1}(x')\,dx',
\]

\[
P_k(x)
=
\frac{
F_k(x)R_k(x)
}{
\int F_k(y)R_k(y)\,dy
}.
\]

Here:

- \(F\) represents source-side preparation and forward propagation;
- \(R\) represents compatibility with later records or boundary conditions;
- \(S\) represents settlement or selection at a later layer;
- \(P\) is the two-boundary conditioned distribution.

This resembles forward-backward smoothing, adjoint-state methods, and pre/postselected quantum mechanics. Aharonov, Bergmann, and Lebowitz showed that ensembles defined by both initial and final conditions admit time-symmetric probability assignments [2]. That mathematical resemblance does not prove the causaliton ontology.

### 4.2 Two interpretations of “propagation”

The phrase “causalitons propagate backward” has at least three possible meanings.

**Physical backward propagation.**  
A real field propagates toward decreasing coordinate time according to a Lorentz-covariant equation. This is the strongest and most demanding version.

**Causal-hierarchy factorization.**  
A global relation between a later record and an earlier source factors through nested intermediate systems:

\[
D\rightarrow E_3\rightarrow E_2\rightarrow E_1\rightarrow S.
\]

“Propagation” is the structured dependence across those layers.

**Consistency-search or solver depth.**  
The universe is an all-at-once history, while backward propagation describes iterations of a mathematical procedure that finds a globally consistent solution.

A mature theory must choose among these or show that they are equivalent descriptions.

### 4.3 Causalitons are not ordinary photons

A photon is a quantized excitation of the electromagnetic field. A causaliton, as presently defined, is associated with the compatibility of possible source-environment-detector records. It may live in Hilbert space or history space rather than ordinary three-dimensional space.

The clean relationship is:

\[
\text{photon state}
+
\text{detector causaliton structure}
\longrightarrow
\text{possible quantized records}.
\]

The photon and causaliton do not “collide” as two conventional particles. Their structures jointly determine which complete histories can close.

## 5. The Three Causaliton Regimes

The three regimes are not three probability magnitudes. They are three proposed statuses of causal closure relative to a detector.

Let \(b\) denote backward causal depth through the admitted hierarchy, and let:

\[
\mathcal K_D(b,\chi)=1
\]

mean that a valid source-record closure for contribution \(\chi\) has been found by depth \(b\).

Define:

\[
b_D^*(\chi)
=
\inf
\left\{
b\ge 0:
\mathcal K_D(b,\chi)=1
\right\}.
\]

### 5.1 Classical causalitons: direct closure

\[
b_D^*=0.
\]

The contribution belongs to a directly localizable source-to-record chain. It supports ordinary event language:

- this atom emitted;
- this detector absorbed;
- this collision transferred momentum;
- this pointer moved;
- this environment redundantly encoded the result.

“Classical” here does not necessarily mean low velocity or nonrelativistic. It means **robustly record-closing**.

### 5.2 Virtual causalitons: finite-backdate closure

\[
0<b_D^*<\infty.
\]

The present relation cannot be represented as a simple direct forward-local chain, but finite backdating reaches an earlier common causal region where closure is possible.

The clearest intended geometry is:

\[
D_A
\longleftarrow
S_{\mathrm{common}}
\longrightarrow
D_B.
\]

The distant outcomes are not caused directly across a spacelike interval. Their relationship closes by backdating to a shared preparation or causal ancestry.

This is related to, but should not be equated with, virtual particles. A virtual particle is an internal term in perturbative calculations, not an independently detected object. A virtual causaliton is defined by finite causal backdating rather than off-shell momentum alone.

Tunneling may eventually become a subclass of the virtual regime:

\[
\text{allowed source region}
\rightarrow
\text{locally nonpropagating segment}
\rightarrow
\text{allowed detector region}.
\]

The endpoints can remain causally related even though the intermediate mode is evanescent. Relativistic analyses require the causal signal front to remain subluminal [20].

### 5.3 Imaginary causalitons: nonclosing support

\[
b_D^*=\infty.
\]

No finite admitted causal ancestry yields an independently record-closing relation for that contribution relative to detector \(D\).

The term **imaginary** is provisional and intentionally analogous to the distinction between phase-bearing mathematical support and ordinary propagating modes. It does not mean “unreal.” It means that the contribution may belong to the complete information-phase structure without possessing a finite source-record closure.

A physically meaningful imaginary causaliton must affect at least one invariant global structure:

- interference;
- joint correlations;
- normalization;
- entanglement;
- a conserved quantity;
- or the amplitudes of record-closing sectors.

If its presence and absence produce exactly identical predictions for every admissible observation, its status becomes metaphysical rather than physical.

### 5.4 The halting-like asymmetry

Virtual causation has a finite witness. When closure is found at depth \(b\), the search terminates.

Imaginary causation generally lacks a finite witness. At any finite depth \(B\), failure establishes only:

\[
\mathcal K_D(b,\chi)=0
\quad
\text{for }b\le B.
\]

It does not establish:

\[
\mathcal K_D(b,\chi)=0
\quad
\text{for all finite }b.
\]

This resembles semidecidability:

- closure can be certified when found;
- permanent nonclosure cannot be inferred from finite failure.

A literal reduction to the Turing halting problem has not been demonstrated. The framework presently claims a logical analogy and asks whether physically realistic causal-closure problems can encode universal computation.

## 6. Existence Without Realization

The most distinctive ontological claim is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{existence is more extensive than realization}.
}
\]

This does not mean that any imagined invisible entity should be declared real. Existence requires positive invariant support. Candidate grounds include:

- unitarity of a closed state;
- gauge-invariant phase relations;
- conservation laws;
- purification structure;
- entanglement constraints;
- spectral identities;
- or changes in observable interference when the contribution is removed.

### 6.1 The uneven epistemic street

Two inferences are invalid:

\[
\text{not observed}
\Rightarrow
\text{does not exist},
\]

and

\[
\text{not disproven}
\Rightarrow
\text{exists}.
\]

The framework rejects both. It instead demands:

- a positive invariant argument for existential support;
- a separate causal-closure argument for realizability;
- and a separate detector model for record probability.

### 6.2 Probability tails and causal vacuity

State normalization says:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1.
\]

It does not by itself say that every nonzero state component has an independently realizable detector history.

Born’s original statistical interpretation was developed in collision and scattering problems [1]. Modern quantum theory assigns probabilities to outcomes of specified measurements. The danger arises in informal speech when one moves directly from:

\[
|\psi(x)|^2>0
\]

to:

> This particular prepared particle can physically appear at \(x\) if we wait long enough.

A nonzero spatial tail can represent state support without supplying a source-attributable detector channel. Positive-energy states can develop arbitrarily distant tails without enabling superluminal signaling [10].

The framework therefore distinguishes:

\[
p_{\mathrm{phase}}
\]

from:

\[
p_{\mathrm{source\rightarrow record}}.
\]

The latter requires a specified source, detector, coupling, spacetime geometry, background model, and record criterion.

### 6.3 Practical, epistemic, and algorithmic vacuity

Tiny probabilities create several different problems.

**Practical vacuity.**  
A known nonzero probability is too small to sample with available resources.

**Epistemic vacuity.**  
The model does not specify whether the detector event is attributable to the source or background.

**Algorithmic vacuity.**  
No general procedure can decide whether finite causal closure exists.

The framework’s strongest claim concerns the second and possibly the third, not the mere numerical smallness of a probability.

### 6.4 Renormalization is not normalization

Normalization ensures that a quantum state or probability distribution is complete. Renormalization addresses scale dependence and divergent expressions in quantum field theory by relating bare and observable parameters or by integrating out inaccessible degrees of freedom. Dyson’s 1949 synthesis of the Tomonaga-Schwinger and Feynman approaches belongs to the historical development of renormalized quantum electrodynamics [19].

Renormalization may eventually be relevant because it studies how eliminated degrees of freedom leave effective traces. It is not the origin of the rule that probabilities sum to one.

## 7. Phase Structure, Group Phase, and Refraction

The original “group wave” language becomes precise by treating it as a collective resultant, not a separate magical object.

Let individual causaliton contributions be:

\[
c_j(x)=a_j(x)e^{i\phi_j(x)}.
\]

Define:

\[
Z(x)=\sum_j c_j(x),
\]

\[
\Phi(x)=\arg Z(x),
\]

\[
\mathcal C(x)
=
\frac{|Z(x)|}{\sum_j a_j(x)}.
\]

Here:

- \(Z\) is the collective resultant;
- \(\Phi\) is the group phase;
- \(\mathcal C\) measures phase concentration.

A richer phase spectrum is:

\[
M_n(x)
=
\frac{
\sum_j a_j(x)e^{in\phi_j(x)}
}{
\sum_j a_j(x)
}.
\]

The group phase is only the first summary of a full distribution.

### 7.1 “Flattening” is detector-relative

Flattening should not mean that the probability distribution becomes perfectly uniform. It means that the inherited record-closing structure becomes less concentrated relative to the detector’s relevant coordinates.

Let \(\mathcal N_D\) be the detector’s effective channel. Distinguishability between two source states is:

\[
\Delta_D(i,j)
=
\frac12
\left\|
\mathcal N_D(\rho_i)-\mathcal N_D(\rho_j)
\right\|_1.
\]

The full states may remain distinct while their detector projections become less distinguishable:

\[
\Delta_D(i,j)\downarrow.
\]

This is a rigorous analogue of phase-space flattening.

### 7.2 Refraction is a separate axis

Refraction should not define whether a causaliton is classical, virtual, or imaginary.

The taxonomy asks:

> What causal-closure status does this contribution possess?

Refraction asks:

> How does the collective phase distribution propagate, disperse, scatter, or lose concentration through an environment?

A causaliton scattering relation might be:

\[
\mathbf c_{\mathrm{out}}
=
S\mathbf c_{\mathrm{in}},
\qquad
S^\dagger S=I.
\]

Information can be preserved while the phase distribution spreads across modes.

### 7.3 Stationary action as causal localization

The path integral assigns phases:

\[
\mathcal A
=
\int \mathcal D\gamma\,
e^{iS[\gamma]/\hbar}.
\]

Classical paths arise near stationary action:

\[
\delta S[\gamma_{\mathrm{cl}}]=0.
\]

The framework interprets this not as nature geometrically searching for the shortest path, but as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{stationary action}
=
\text{maximal robustness of causal localization under nearby phase variations}.
}
\]

Near a stationary history, neighboring contributions reinforce. Away from it, rapidly varying phases cancel, making causal attribution less concentrated.

The Aharonov-Bohm effect is a critical constraint: phase structure depends on a global gauge relation even in regions where the local magnetic field vanishes. Electron-holography experiments confirmed the phase shift with shielded flux [14]. Therefore causalitons cannot be merely local rays carrying forces. Any formal theory must be gauge invariant and history-sensitive.

## 8. Observers Within Observers

An observer is any physical system that acquires stable relational information about another system. Consciousness is not required.

The hierarchy may be represented as:

\[
S
\rightarrow
E_1
\rightarrow
E_2
\rightarrow
\cdots
\rightarrow
D.
\]

Examples include:

- an electron and nucleus;
- a photon and cavity;
- neighboring molecules;
- a crystal lattice;
- a beam splitter;
- a detector atom;
- an amplifier;
- a computer memory;
- an experimentalist.

### 8.1 Environmental constraint is not decoherence

The environment can play at least three roles.

**Mode shaping.**  
A potential, boundary, field, or lattice changes the allowed coherent solutions without distinguishing a particular history.

**History differentiation.**  
Possible histories become correlated with different environment states:

\[
\sum_\gamma
A_\gamma|\gamma\rangle|E_0\rangle
\longrightarrow
\sum_\gamma
A_\gamma|\gamma\rangle|E_\gamma\rangle.
\]

Interference between \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma'\) is weighted by:

\[
\Gamma_{\gamma\gamma'}
=
\langle E_{\gamma'}|E_\gamma\rangle.
\]

**Settlement and redundancy.**  
Many environmental fragments independently encode the same distinction. The overlap between macroscopic alternatives becomes effectively negligible.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{environmental constraint}
\neq
\text{decoherence}
\neq
\text{final realization}.
}
\]

### 8.2 The electron in an atom

A free electron and a bound electron share mass, charge, spin, and statistics. Their available modes differ because the relational environment differs.

\[
H_{\mathrm{free}}
=
\frac{\mathbf p^2}{2m},
\]

\[
H_{\mathrm{atom}}
=
\frac{\mathbf p^2}{2m}
-
\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}.
\]

The nucleus does not need to measure the electron’s exact path. It shapes the coherent joint solution space.

In causaliton language:

> The atomic environment supplies partial relational constraints that limit which later detector closures are compatible with the electron-nucleus system.

### 8.3 Partial realization cones

For each environmental system \(E_k\), define:

\[
\mathcal R_{E_k}
=
\{\gamma:
C_{E_k}[\gamma]\neq 0\}.
\]

The total compatible history space is approximately:

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{total}}
=
\mathcal R_S
\cap
\mathcal R_{E_1}
\cap
\cdots
\cap
\mathcal R_D.
\]

These are not new relativistic light cones. They are detector- and subsystem-relative compatibility regions constrained by actual local interactions.

### 8.4 Local quantum measurement as a benchmark

Relativistic quantum measurement frameworks model a probe as another quantum field coupled locally to the system in a bounded spacetime region. The induced system observables are localized in the causal hull of the interaction, and causally disjoint measurements must factor consistently [7]. Recent work explicitly studies moving the measurement cut through successive probes, closely resembling the “observers within observers” hierarchy [8].

Any causaliton theory must reproduce this causal factorization.

## 9. A Reconstruction of Wave-Particle Duality

The framework replaces one ambiguous duality with a layered relation.

\[
\boxed{
\text{wave}
=
\text{phase structure among possible source-environment-record histories},
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{particle event}
=
\text{a quantized closure of one source-environment-detector relation}.
}
\]

A quantum field excitation remains quantized. What is denied is that the excitation must already possess one complete classical trajectory before a record exists.

### 9.1 Double slit

Let:

\[
c_L(x)=a_L(x)e^{i\phi_L(x)},
\qquad
c_R(x)=a_R(x)e^{i\phi_R(x)}.
\]

Then:

\[
P(x)=|c_L(x)+c_R(x)|^2.
\]

The interference pattern belongs to the phase relations among possible detector records. The final spot is a localized quantized closure.

With which-path states:

\[
c_L|M_L\rangle+c_R|M_R\rangle,
\]

the cross term is weighted by:

\[
\langle M_R|M_L\rangle.
\]

The loss of visible interference is caused by distinguishable records, not consciousness.

### 9.2 Delayed choice and quantum erasure

Delayed-choice experiments do not require a particle to decide retroactively whether it “was a wave” or “was a particle.” They show that the complete record structure determines which interference relations are visible.

Delayed-choice entanglement swapping can project already recorded photons into entangled or separable conditional ensembles depending on a later joint measurement [11]. In causaliton language, the later record does not rewrite local clicks. It closes a joint relationship by finite backdating to the common preparation.

Quantum erasure similarly changes which distinctions become available in the final record basis. No visible past distribution is rewritten.

### 9.3 Interaction-free measurement

Interaction-free measurement reveals the presence of an object in trials without absorption [12]. The object changes which histories remain phase compatible. A blocked or absorption-capable history need not become a separately realized event to alter the distribution of record-closing outcomes.

This is one of the clearest standard analogues of a “virtual cause”:

\[
\text{no separately localized transfer}
\quad
\text{but}
\quad
\text{a measurable distributional consequence}.
\]

### 9.4 Weak measurement and reversal

A weak measurement creates a partial record. Under suitable conditions it can be probabilistically undone, restoring an unknown qubit state [13].

This supports a graded hierarchy:

\[
\text{correlation}
\rightarrow
\text{partial record}
\rightarrow
\text{redundant settlement}.
\]

It argues against treating “measurement” as one instantaneous metaphysical action.

### 9.5 Hong-Ou-Mandel interference

Two-photon interference shows that causaliton contributions must attach to possible **joint records**, not independently labeled particle paths. The alternatives:

\[
A\rightarrow1,\ B\rightarrow2
\]

and

\[
A\rightarrow2,\ B\rightarrow1
\]

interfere in configuration space. A causaliton theory built from separately sampled miniature rays would fail.

### 9.6 Stern-Gerlach and internal state space

A Stern-Gerlach experiment shows that causaliton phase structure cannot live only in ordinary position space. It must include internal Hilbert-space degrees of freedom. Sequential Stern-Gerlach experiments reveal noncommuting record structures, not ignorance about one classical spin vector.

### 9.7 Photoelectric and Compton effects

The wave structure determines amplitudes among possible records. The particle aspect appears in localized quantized transfer:

- discrete detector excitations;
- conserved energy and momentum;
- electron emission;
- recoil.

This cleanly separates phase propagation from event realization.

## 15. Black Holes as an Extreme Stress Test

Black holes sharply separate local realization from external recordability.

\boxed{

It does not prove retrocausality. It shows that “particles already sitting everywhere” is not the only natural relativistic ontology.

This sharpens the distinction:

\[
\text{information preserved}
\neq
\text{information locally or simply decodable}.

Black-hole information is therefore a strong analogue for existential support without easy realization, but not necessarily for absolutely unconfirmable imaginary causation.

### 21.9 Uncontrolled flexibility

A framework capable of redescribing every outcome after the fact but predicting none is not a theory.

## 24. Conclusion

The causaliton framework is most likely not literally correct in its current form. That is not a defect to conceal; it is the premise under which the framework should be used.

Its present achievement is a form of conceptual compression. It replaces the undifferentiated phrase “wave-particle duality” with a layered architecture:

\[
\text{phase-supported existence}
\rightarrow
\text{causal compatibility}
\rightarrow
\text{record closure}
\rightarrow
\text{redundant classical reality}.
\]

It distinguishes global information preservation from local causal verification. It explains why an environment can constrain a quantum system without fully measuring it. It interprets a particle as a quantized relational closure rather than a classical object secretly following one path. It offers a forward-time intuition for probability and a hierarchy-based intuition for collapse and classicality. It separates spacelike correlation from signaling and finite backdating from impossible frame changes. It turns black holes, scrambling, delayed choice, weak measurement, tunneling, and accelerated detectors into stress tests of the same conceptual boundaries.

Most importantly, it generates questions that are sharper than the intuitions from which it began:

- Does nonzero phase support always correspond to a realizable detector history?
- What physical process turns a correlation into a record?
- Is causal closure graded, delayed, or computationally complex?
- Can future-conditioned constraint possess a finite invariant response scale?
- Does temporary branch expansion matter after exact reversal?

- Can a nonclosing sector influence observable interference?
- Is the virtual-imaginary boundary algorithmically decidable?
- Can realization density reproduce proper time?
- Does the causaliton disappear when the correct mathematics is written?

A useful speculative framework does not need to be the final ontology. It must make ignorance structured. It must reveal where metaphors break, where equations are required, and where experiments can force a choice.

By that standard, Two-Boundary Constraint Dynamics has become valuable enough to preserve, formalize, criticize, and test.
