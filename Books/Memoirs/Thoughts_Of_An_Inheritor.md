no one chose when they are born. There is a conceppt for this which historians and philosphers see fit to be called generational guilt or the burden of abundance. For the past centuries, every generations primary goal was to see through the next generation, given crises' such as WWII and the Cold War, their efforts was placed in building a safer and more stable world so their children wouldn't have to face the horrors they did in their time. Their win condition was this time, something that looked like right here and right now. Of course, one could say abundance brings its own "rotting" weight. Widespread burnout, alienation, abstract existential dreads, especially when put up with cognitive forces that outstrip one's sensibilites and delierations. That creates a massive psychological weight soceity is struggling to keep up with. But the world is nonetheless build on massive past sacrificies, so therefore one should work to pay forward the debt to the past.

In this light, one would aptly feelss the weight of "the responsibility of the inheritor", that one has to earn the peacetime, discard frivooulous thoughts like for what purpose does one exist and instead define and ground meaning through the sacrifices the previous generation made. Living a frivolous llife might seem like a disrespect of that foundation but in a way, one must realize that the previous generation sacrificed so one could lead frivolous lives, fulfilling only to themselves and their wishes. Previous generations dug trenches, built factories and survived the physical and then phscological brink of annihilation for years so we wouldn't have to. One must see it a responsibility therefore, not necessarily to sacrifcce as they did but as caretakers of a protected future and massive leverage of technology, education and relative peace handed down. When previous generations sacrificed, they likely would have preferred that future genertions didn't feel they had to owe them and preferred they jst be free. A true gift, where those who suffered to build a free world wherein lightheartedness was bought and hard won. Survivers of the Great Depression or the venerable soldiers of the Great Wars rarely intend for their children "I want them to carry a heavy psychological burden of debt to me", but rather are steadfast in their efforts to their children of "I want them to never know hunger", "I want them to have choices I never had and to never make the hard choices I had to make". For much of us, embracing this of course means embracing frivolity, embracing freedom of thought, pursuing the arts. 

 To my mind, however, when it comes to motivation, I would soonest part with a sum to bet that one, if one could supposedly feel what it was like to have fought in a war, been in that era and transported here, there would be much intense motivation far beyond the standard "pschological" or nurture kind.
