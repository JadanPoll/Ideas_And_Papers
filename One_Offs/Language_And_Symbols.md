Now in conversation with chatgpt for some clarity, there are 4 mechanisms:
Symbolic Manipulation: Can I follow transformations without translating every transformation into another representational system?
Symbolic Binding: A new symbol needs an already stable anchor. Translation is perfectly reasonable as a bootstrap mechanism. 
However "continuing to attach increasingly complicated French structures to English representations even after French itself is capable of supporting those structures" while it works obstructs native binding to the language. An analogous mathematical failure would be constantly thinking $\forall x \in X$ as an English sentence "for every x belonging to X..." even after encountering it a 10,000 times,
English becomes a crutch preventing native binding
3. Context mutation: Learning not just the thing but the statistical ecology surrounding the thing. Encountering enough 
Structurally varying instances around the invariant that it becomes perceptually implicit. Like "the" and "and"

So in one way the goal is not "I know a lot of mathematics or language X", or "I can solve lots of problems"(although being able to do that certainly certifies whats coming next since your brain will not fail to pick up the statistical context, but reason this is not enough is because of stuff like binding etc) but "I  can represent a large amount of mathematical/symbolic/linguistical structure in a small amount of working memory."

An expert proof may contain 200 symbolic operations but the expert really only consciously experiences more like 10-20 chunks on reading and maybe 50-80 on writing.
